# Pressure-driven temperature variations in open boreholes

In air-filled boreholes the day-to-day temperature scatter reaches 43 mK at 21 m (B40, Kohnen),
compared with 0.1 mK in a snow-filled hole. The scatter is not instrument noise: it is a single
pressure-driven signal, T′(z,t) = b(z)·F(t), with F ≈ dp/dt.

(i) **Above firn close-off: advection.** Barometric pressure changes pump air through the hole,
25–400 times the volume of the hole's own air column; the hole feeds the surrounding firn. The
displaced air carries the temperature of its origin and relaxes to the wall within τ = 10–15 min
(95-s data, Fig. a, b). Daily sampling therefore sees T ∝ dp/dt, with amplitude set by the local
temperature gradient.

(ii) **Below close-off: compression heat of the hole air.** The wall is sealed, advection stops,
and b drops by an order of magnitude to 0.012–0.026 mK per hPa/day, positive despite a reversed
gradient. Adiabatic heating of the air, lost to the wall within ~45 s, predicts 0.025–0.05 without
a free parameter (Fig. c).

(iii) **Filled holes:** the heat is stored in the ice matrix, T ∝ p with b = φ/(ρc) ≈ 0.03 mK/hPa.

**Removing b(z)·F(t) reduces the scatter 8–16× at 26–50 m and to 0.02–0.03 mK below close-off (Fig. d);
tested against a snow-filled hole at GRIP, the error drops 4–10×.** The mean profile is unaffected
(< 0.1 mK). For 2.8 mK accuracy, fill to ~60 m, or to ~25 m with pressure correction.
